Our Founders
Picture passport sized - Haji Inam Ellahi Asar (Late)
Link his name to the following content on a separate page
Born in October 1927 in Chiniot, he devoted more than five decades of his life to the service of humanity, earning deep respect as a distinguished social worker and philanthropist.
A highly successful businessman, he was recognized among the highest taxpayers in Pakistan during his prime years, reflecting his integrity, vision, and contribution to the nation’s economy.
As the Founder and Honorary Project Director of Hijaz Hospital, he transformed his lifelong commitment to compassion and public welfare into a lasting institution dedicated to healthcare and human service.
With a generous contribution exceeding PKR 500 million — including valuable personal properties — he selflessly supported numerous welfares, healthcare, and charitable initiatives for the betterment of society.
He remained actively associated with more than 30 welfare organizations, serving in leadership, advisory, and guiding roles with remarkable dedication and sincerity.
In recognition of his extraordinary humanitarian services and lifelong devotion to social welfare, the Hijaz Welfare Society proudly recommended his name for a prestigious Civil Award.
Picture passport sized - Mian Abdul Waheed (Late)
Link his name to the following content on a separate page
Born in April 1931 in East Punjab, he was a distinguished humanitarian admired for his integrity, humility, and lifelong dedication to public welfare and community service.
As a founding member and one of the principal driving forces behind the establishment of Hijaz Hospital, alongside Haji Inam Elahi Asar, he played a pivotal role in shaping the institution’s mission of compassionate healthcare and social service.
From its humble beginning as a dispensary in 1979, he remained actively and personally involved in every stage of the hospital’s journey — from planning and construction to its continuous growth and expansion.
Through his visionary leadership, tireless efforts, and unwavering commitment, Hijaz Hospital evolved into a modern 200-bedded healthcare institution serving thousands with dignity and care.
Beyond healthcare, he was actively associated with several renowned welfare organizations, including Defence Welfare Society, Fountain House, Lions Club, and Akhuwat, contributing generously through leadership, guidance, and humanitarian support.
Today, Hijaz Hospital stands as a lasting symbol of his vision, dedication, and extraordinary service to humanity — a legacy that continues to inspire generations.
